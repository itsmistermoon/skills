# Viral Content Formula

Recipe for scroll-stopping pieces: **false belief → hook → truth shown**. People ignore what they "already know", but when you show them that something they believed is wrong, they're hooked, wanting to know the truth.

## Steps

### 1. Find the false belief

Look for what the audience believes about the product or the campaign that is false, or that underestimates what's being offered. Starting point: real customer phrases like "oh, I didn't know that...".

- ❌ "A personalized notebook is just a name slapped on it" → ✅ it's one of the few gifts that gets used every day for months
- ❌ "Personalizing always costs more" → ✅ the cheap generic gift gets thrown away; the personalized one gets used and not forgotten
- ❌ "Everything's digital now, paper is useless" → ✅ handwriting helps you remember better than typing

**Done when:** you have a false-belief/truth pair, and the truth can be demonstrated with the real product.

### 2. Open with the false belief

Put the viewer first in the "I already know this" position; the answer comes after. In video, the hook goes spoken or written on screen before second 3 — no thumbnail will save you.

- Video: "If you think a personalized notebook is just slapping a name on it... watch this."
- Text: "3 things people believe about personalized gifts... that are wrong."

**Done when:** the hook is in the first 2-3 seconds (video) or the first line (text), framed from the false belief.

### 3. Alternate human and technical

Dole out the truth gradually, alternating **human/emotional** beats (who receives it, the reaction, a real story) with **technical/process** beats (how it's made, the materials, the detail).

Reel structure: hook (0-3 s) → personalization process → reaction on receiving it → close-up detail → closing ("this is what people are actually buying") + CTA.

**Done when:** the structure alternates at least once between an emotional beat and a technical one.

### 4. Verify the promise (legitbait gate)

The hook opens the curiosity; the product closes it. **Legitbait** is a hook that faithfully represents what follows; its opposite, clicktrap, promises what isn't delivered. Every claim in the hook must be fulfilled by what is actually delivered or shown — a broken promise produces bad reviews and returns.

**Done when:** every promise in the hook is backed by the real product/content.

### 5. Adapt to the platform

Place the hook where the target platform reads it, per the reference table.

**Done when:** the hook's position matches the table for that platform.

### 6. Deliver 2-3 alternative hooks

There is no perfect hook on the first try. Deliver 2-3 versions to test: same piece, different packaging. If it underperforms in the first days, change the hook without touching the content — it's the cheapest intervention. The flywheel: better hook → higher CTR → the platform shows you to more people.

**Done when:** there are 2-3 distinct hooks for the same piece.

## Reference

### Where the hook goes per platform

| Platform | Where the hook goes | What the algorithm measures |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | Title + thumbnail | Clicks and watching the full video |
| TikTok / Reels | First 1-3 s of the video | % of the video watched |
| Instagram | First 1-3 s, with on-screen text (many watch without sound) | DM shares and saves; these outweigh likes |

### Quick template

> "People think _______________ but in reality _______________"

The truth is shown, not just said. Each completed sentence is a piece ready to produce.

### Hook calibration

- The strongest hook lives right at the edge between honest and exaggerated — get close to the legitbait/clicktrap edge without crossing it.
- Technical specificity alienates: "A5 stationery with hand-sewn endpapers" only speaks to insiders; "the gift people actually use every day" speaks to everyone. Swap the technical term for the benefit or the question behind it.
- Clarity can beat loudness: a simple, direct title can outperform a flashy one. If the hook sounds vague, try the direct version before the more "salesy" one.

Main source: Derek Muller (Veritasium), ["Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective"](https://youtu.be/S2xHZPH5Sng).
